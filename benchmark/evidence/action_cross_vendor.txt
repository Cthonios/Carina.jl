Matrix-free stiffness action across three vendors: the FP64 reading retired
===========================================================================

Problem: torsion-qs deck (benchmark/inputs/torsion-qs-gpu-cg-jacobi.yaml),
530,523 DOF / 527,877 free, 160,000 HEX8 -- the same problem as
benchmark/evidence/action_ablation.txt.  Driver: benchmark/action_bench.jl,
which times `stiffness_action` against `mass_action` in one process.

BOTH ROWS BELOW WERE MEASURED AT THE SAME COMMIT (c85390d, 2026-09-08).  That
matters: an earlier version of this file compared the L4 against the RX 7600
arm in action_ablation.txt, which was taken 2026-08-06 at commit 553b46a --
sixteen days and two kernel commits earlier.  The register/spill work of
053b490 and 85928c5 (2026-08-22) changed the kernel substantially and
unequally across vendors, so that comparison was not commit-matched and its
figures (1.87x, 2.86x) were overstated.  The corrected figures are 1.71x and
2.50x, and the conclusion is unchanged.

  RX 7600  AMD Navi 33, RDNA3, 32 CU, 8 GB, 288 GB/s, FP64 at 1/32 = 0.68 TFLOP/s
           ROCm via AMDGPU.jl, host sirius, Julia 1.12.7
  L4       NVIDIA AD104, Ada, 58 SM, 23 GB, 300 GB/s, FP64 at 1/64 = 0.489 TFLOP/s
           CUDA 13.3 via CUDA.jl artifacts, driver 610.57.04, host rigel, Julia 1.12.7

The L4 is close to a controlled experiment on the FP64 question: LOWER FP64
peak than the RX 7600 (0.489 vs 0.68) at nearly equal memory bandwidth
(300 vs 288 GB/s).  An FP64-throughput-bound kernel must therefore run SLOWER
on it; a bandwidth-bound one must tie.

The deformed state reproduces the initial-state reading: 1.73x on the
stiffness action, 2.50x on the arithmetic part, memory floor within 4%.


1. NUMERICAL AGREEMENT IS EXACT ACROSS VENDORS.  In both states and for both
   kernels, the two cards return identical checksums to every digit printed:
   1.906160379496682e+06 and 1.906165947916275e+06 for the stiffness action
   at U = 0 and at finite strain, and 2.124830956604735e-06 for the mass
   action, each a sum of |.| over 5.3e5 entries.  The KernelAbstractions
   kernels are numerically identical on ROCm and CUDA.  This is the strongest
   available support for the portability claim in benchmark_report.md
   section 8.

2. FP64 PEAK DOES NOT BOUND THIS KERNEL.  Were it the binding ceiling, the L4
   -- with 28% less FP64 peak -- would run the stiffness action at
   8.721 * 0.68/0.489 = 12.13 ms.  It runs it in 5.095 ms: 1.71x faster, and
   2.38x off the prediction.

3. THE MEMORY FLOOR IS THE CONTROL, AND IT HELD.  `mass_action` has the
   identical connectivity gather, the identical 24-DOF atomic scatter, the
   identical element count and launch geometry, and negligible arithmetic.
   It came out within 5% across the two cards (2.488 vs 2.604 ms; 55.1 vs
   52.6 GB/s achieved), as their near-equal bandwidths predict.  The entire
   stiffness difference therefore sits in the arithmetic part, where the L4
   is 2.50x faster (6.233 vs 2.491 ms).

4. THE FLOP BUDGET IS SELF-CONTRADICTORY.  benchmark_report.md section 6 put
   the RX 7600's arithmetic at 40-50% of its 0.68 TFLOP/s FP64 peak.  Any
   figure in that range implies a FLOP count that the L4 would have to
   execute at 139-174% of its own 0.489 TFLOP/s peak, which is impossible.
   The FLOP count behind the 40-50% estimate is too high by at least ~1.4x,
   and it was an assumed count rather than a measured one.

5. THIS CORROBORATES A PROFILE-BASED REFUTATION THAT CAME FIRST.  The A100
   cross-vendor check of 2026-08-22 (benchmark/crosscode/README.md section 4)
   had already refuted the same reading, and by a more direct route: a CUPTI
   profile counted only ~1,100 FP64 arithmetic instructions in the kernel
   against 253 registers per thread, 12.5% occupancy and a ~4 KB spill frame,
   and concluded the kernel was "nowhere near flop-bound".  That refutation
   was never propagated into section 6, which is how the FP64 reading
   survived in the report until now.  The L4 reaches the same conclusion from
   the opposite direction -- hardware rather than instruction counts -- on a
   card whose FP64 peak is lower than the reference.

6. WHAT BINDS THE RX 7600 IS STILL OPEN.  The crosscode section 4 follow-up
   concluded, after the register fix moved the A100 from 7.17 to 2.88 ms and
   the RX 7600 only from 9.36 to 8.66, that "RDNA3 was never spill-bound --
   its 1/32-rate FP64 arithmetic is its wall".  The L4 does not support that
   either: a card with a 1/64 rate and a lower absolute FP64 peak runs the
   same kernel 1.71x faster.  Whatever limits the RX 7600 here, it is not
   FP64 peak in any form that transfers across vendors.  Settling it needs a
   ROCm-side profile of the kind CUPTI provided on the A100, which is open
   item 9 in section 7.

7. THE ARITHMETIC SHARE IS A PROPERTY OF THE CARD, NOT OF THE KERNEL.
   71.5% on the RX 7600, 48.9% on the L4, from the same source at the same
   commit.  Optimizations aimed at the arithmetic will repay at very
   different rates on different hardware, which is the practical consequence
   for section 7 items 5 and 6.
