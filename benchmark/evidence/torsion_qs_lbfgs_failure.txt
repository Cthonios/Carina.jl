L-BFGS on quasi-static torsion: why it fails, on BOTH devices
==============================================================

Case: torsion-qs (530,523 DOF, neo-Hookean, 0.05 rad twist over 4 load steps).
Solver: Newton with `linear solver: type: lbfgs`, history size 10, cap 500
nonlinear iterations.  Measured 2026-08-07 on both backends.

THIS IS NOT A GPU FAILURE.  Both arms were run; they fail the same way, at the
same place, for the same reason.  Earlier revisions of the report listed only
"GPU L-BFGS: fails", because GPU was the only arm that had been measured, and
that phrasing wrongly implied the device was involved.


The two runs are numerically identical where it matters
--------------------------------------------------------
First iterations, |R| (GPU left, CPU right -- same digits):

    iter 1   2.92e+03   2.92e+03
    iter 2   2.45e+03   2.45e+03
    iter 3   2.22e+03   2.22e+03
    iter 4   1.44e+03   1.44e+03
    iter 5   1.16e+03   1.16e+03
    iter 9   9.20e+02   9.20e+02
    iter 11  6.15e+02   6.15e+02

Terminal state after the 500-iteration cap:

    GPU  iter 500  |R| = 6.03e+00  |r| = 1.23e-03  |ΔU| = 1.12e-05  step = 3.91e-03  LS = 9
    CPU  iter 500  |R| = 6.13e+00  |r| = 1.25e-03  |ΔU| = 3.90e-04  step = 1.25e-01  LS = 4

Both stall at |r| ~ 1.2e-3 against a target of 1e-10 relative / 1e-6 absolute
-- roughly seven orders short -- and neither has descended in the preceding
tens of iterations (GPU: 5.87, 5.87, 6.03, 6.01, 6.07, 6.03; CPU: 6.14, 6.09,
6.07, 6.05, 6.69, 6.13).  They oscillate.


Mechanism
---------
1. |r| falls 1.0 -> 0.1 within ~13 iterations, then crawls.  The easy,
   well-conditioned part of the error goes quickly; what remains does not.

2. The line search collapses.  By the end, iterations need LS = 8-10 Armijo
   backtracks to find any acceptable step, accepting step ~1e-3 and moving
   |ΔU| ~ 1e-5.  The quasi-Newton direction has stopped being usefully
   downhill, so the search cuts it to nearly nothing.

3. Root cause: quasi-static has no mass term.  L-BFGS builds an inverse-tangent
   model from the last 10 secant pairs.  On THIS system, CG+Jacobi cannot
   converge within 1000 iterations and even CPU AMG needs 787 -- so the
   operator being modelled is one that Krylov methods need hundreds of
   iterations to invert.  Ten rank-one updates cannot represent it.

4. Contrast with Newmark, where L-BFGS is the fastest GPU option (109 s):
   there the effective operator is K + c_M*M with c_M = 1/(beta*dt^2) ~ 1.6e9
   at dt = 5e-5.  That shift makes the system strongly diagonally dominant --
   close to the identity after Jacobi scaling -- and a low-rank quasi-Newton
   model captures it well, converging in 284-312 iterations per step.  Same
   solver, same code, same device.  Remove the mass term and it stalls.


The hard error is a separate, cosmetic layer
---------------------------------------------
L-BFGS exhausts its 500-iteration cap on the FIRST of four load steps.  Newton
reports step failure; adaptive stepping would normally halve dt and retry, but
the benchmark deck leaves it disabled, so the run raises:

    ERROR: LoadError: Step failed but decrease_factor = 1.0 (adaptive stepping
    disabled). Specify minimum/maximum time step and decrease/increase factors.

    [1] error(s::String)
    [2] _decrease_step!   @ src/integrators.jl:698 [inlined]
    [3] _advance_one_step! @ src/simulation.jl:424
    [4] _subcycle!         @ src/simulation.jl:380
    [5] evolve!            @ src/simulation.jl:333

With adaptive stepping enabled this would not crash -- it would keep halving dt
and either crawl forward at enormous cost or bottom out at min_dt.  The stall,
not the error, is the finding.

Neither run writes a JSONL record: the harness only appends on success.
