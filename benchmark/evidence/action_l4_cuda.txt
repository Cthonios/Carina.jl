Matrix-free stiffness action on NVIDIA L4 (CUDA): the cross-vendor control
==========================================================================

Problem: identical to benchmark/evidence/action_ablation.txt -- torsion-qs deck
(benchmark/inputs/torsion-qs-gpu-cg-jacobi.yaml), 530,523 DOF / 527,877 free,
160,000 HEX8.  Host: rigel (dual EPYC 9634), NVIDIA L4 (AD104, Ada, 58 SM,
23 GB GDDR6, 300 GB/s peak, FP64 at 1/64 rate = 0.489 TFLOP/s), driver
610.57.04 / CUDA 13.3, CUDA.jl artifact runtime 13.3.0, Julia 1.12.7.
Date: 2026-09-08.  Carina 3b75810 + the action_bench backend generalization.

Driver: benchmark/action_bench.jl 50 <state> cuda.  Same fixed direction
vector (1e-6 * randn(MersenneTwister(20260806), n)), same 5 discarded warmup
reps, same 50 timed reps, same median.  The only change to the driver is that
the backend is resolved from an argument instead of hard-coded to ROCm.

Why this run exists: benchmark_report.md section 6 concluded from the RX 7600
alone that the action is "FP64-compute-bound", at "roughly 40-50% of this
card's FP64 peak", and that "FP64 throughput is the binding ceiling".  That is
a single-vendor reading.  The L4 is the natural control for it: its FP64 peak
is LOWER than the RX 7600's (0.489 vs 0.68 TFLOP/s) while its memory bandwidth
is nearly the same (300 vs 288 GB/s), so an FP64-bound kernel must run SLOWER
on it, and a bandwidth-bound one must run at the same speed.

1. NUMERICAL AGREEMENT ACROSS VENDORS.  The stiffness checksum is
   1.906160379497e+06 on ROCm (arm A, full precision) and
   1.906160379496682e+06 on CUDA -- agreement to 13 significant digits on a
   sum over 5.3e5 entries.  The mass checksum agrees to every digit printed.
   The KernelAbstractions kernels are numerically equivalent across the two
   vendors, which is the strongest available statement of the portability
   claim in section 8.

2. THE FP64-BOUND READING DOES NOT SURVIVE.  If FP64 throughput were the
   binding ceiling, the L4 -- with 28% LESS FP64 peak -- would run the
   stiffness action at 9.503 * 0.68/0.489 = 13.2 ms.  It runs it in 5.095 ms,
   faster by 1.87x and off the prediction by 2.6x.

3. THE MEMORY FLOOR IS THE CONTROL, AND IT HELD.  `mass_action` -- identical
   gather, identical 24-DOF atomic scatter, identical launch geometry,
   negligible arithmetic -- came out within 10% across the two cards
   (2.377 vs 2.604 ms; 57.6 vs 52.6 GB/s achieved).  Their memory subsystems
   are equivalent for this access pattern, so the entire stiffness difference
   sits in the arithmetic part, where it is 2.86x.

4. THE FLOP BUDGET IS JOINTLY INCONSISTENT.  Section 6 puts the RX 7600's
   arithmetic at 40-50% of its 0.68 TFLOP/s FP64 peak.  Take the midpoint:
   0.306 TFLOP/s over the 7.126 ms arithmetic part is 2.18e9 FLOP, i.e.
   ~13.6 kFLOP/element.  The same FLOP count on the L4's 2.491 ms arithmetic
   part demands 0.876 TFLOP/s -- 179% of that card's 0.489 TFLOP/s FP64 peak,
   which is impossible.  The two cards cannot both be near their FP64 peaks on
   a shared FLOP count, so the FLOP budget behind the 40-50% figure is too
   high by at least ~2x and the "binding ceiling" reading is not supported by
   it.  Whether the RX 7600 is FP64-limited and the L4 is limited by something
   else, or neither is, cannot be settled without an actual FLOP count or the
   occupancy/register profiling that section 7 item 9 already lists as open.

5. THE VENDOR-DEPENDENT SPLIT IS ITSELF THE FINDING.  Arithmetic is 75% of the
   action on the RX 7600 and 49% on the L4.  "The kernel is FP64-compute-bound"
   is not a property of the kernel; it was a property of the one card it was
   measured on.  Any optimization targeted at the arithmetic (section 7 item 5,
   ablating the explicit kernel; item 6, smoother tuning) will pay back at very
   different rates on different hardware.
